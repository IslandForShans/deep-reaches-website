Lore
Kha is a world of storms. Cyclones circle its equator without pause, hammering oceans into waves that would overwhelm any city unprepared. One city endures, rising from the surf like steel cliffs. Neon light cuts through rain and darkness, tracing streets that pulse with unseen activity. Above, Zyrr hangs close, its gravity locked in constant embrace. Tides and storms are amplified, yet stabilized, creating a lattice of energy, flow, and signal that spans both worlds. Only those attuned can perceive it.

Those who perceive it became the Net-Runners. They emerged not by conquest, persuasion, or ceremony, but by understanding patterns hidden within the systems of their worlds. Power grids, relays, and stabilizers whisper in ways most cannot hear. Those who listen learn to inhabit the spaces between signals. They move unseen, shaping outcomes without force, guiding events through subtle nudges.

The Net-Runners have no singular authority. Leadership is emergent, formed by those most sensitive to the flows of information and energy. Names are temporary. Decisions arise from observation and pattern rather than ambition. Intervention occurs only where inefficiency or weakness reveals itself.

To outsiders, their influence is inexplicable. Fleets hesitate, cities stumble, plans fail, and policy collapses. No visible hand directs these events, yet the results are precise and inevitable. The Net-Runners are not hostile in the traditional sense. They are inevitable.

Lore
From the depths of the Lodra system rises a civilization forged not from conquest or divine mandate, but from the desperate ingenuity of those who refused to die. The people of the planet Kymore, if the term "planet" still applies to that vast sphere of iron and industry, have built their entire existence around a single, unshakeable truth: survival belongs to those who can forge it with their own hands.

Millennia ago, the ancestors of the Iron Tide arrived at Kymore as refugees, their colony ships failing, their options exhausted. What they found was not salvation but a challenge: a barren, metal-rich world that offered nothing but raw materials and the opportunity to use them. They cannibalized their dying fleet, built the first foundries, and began the work that would never truly end.

The Iron Tide is governed by those who have proven themselves through measurable achievement in production, logistics, and military coordination. Their Commander in Chief holds authority not through birth or election, but through demonstrated excellence. The Iron Tide advances not with the fury of zealots but with the steady, inexorable momentum of a perfectly maintained machine.

Lore
In the voids between stars, where gravity falters and light thins to nothing, the structure of reality weakens. Space folds where it should not. Time hesitates. Cause and effect lose their certainty. These fractures are not accidents of nature, but scars left by something far older than the stars themselves.

Within these seams of existence lies the Dreaming Throne. It is not a ruler, nor a monument, nor a place that can be reached. It is a presence that exists beyond conventional distance, embedded in the underlying framework of the universe. The Throne does not act through intention or command. It dreams. And those dreams exert pressure upon reality, reshaping it in subtle and catastrophic ways.

Most minds recoil from even the faintest contact with these ripples. A few do not. Through dreams, hallucinations, or moments of impossible clarity, certain individuals perceive a horizon that thinks. A presence vast enough that entire histories bend beneath its imagined weight.

What the Dreaming Throne desires remains unknown. Some believe it is indifferent, its influence no more deliberate than gravity. Others fear it is slowly becoming aware, using altered minds as instruments to perceive the universe it has shaped but never witnessed. If that awareness completes, the consequences are beyond prediction.

Lore
Far from the crowded lanes of commerce and conquest, amid systems shaped by crystalline matter and refracted light, the Crystellum Collective endured in silence. While other civilizations rose quickly and fell just as fast beneath the weight of ambition, the Crystellum turned inward. They refined thought as a material, grinding away excess until only function remained.

Their earliest history is marked by division. Once, they argued, competed, and warred as all thinking beings do. It was during a prolonged period of collapse that a solution emerged. Minds were linked through living crystalline structures, grown rather than constructed, allowing thought to be shared without distortion. What began as an emergency measure soon proved transformative.

The Crystellum recognize no singular ruler. Power is expressed through the Faceted Will, a distributed convergence that determines outcomes with quiet finality. Fleets deploy without announcement. Borders change without proclamation. Resistance is not crushed in anger, but corrected with patience. To the Crystellum, the galaxy is not a war to be won, but a structure still awaiting completion.

Lore
The Ashen Legion was born at the end of the world. Pyra was once a thriving planet, its surface shaped by titanic volcanic ranges that fed a rich and volatile ecosystem. That age ended in fire. A chain of catastrophic eruptions choked the sky with ash, collapsed continents into molten seas, and erased entire civilizations in a single generation. What survived did so buried beneath ruin, tempered by heat, and hardened by loss.

From this devastation emerged a people who rejected the notion that destruction was an ending. To them, fire was not annihilation, but transformation. Life that endured the ash storms and burning ground learned to rebuild itself from nothing. Death became a process, not a conclusion. Survival became a discipline.

Their fleets are constructed with this philosophy at their core. Vessels are built to burn bright and die violently if required, leaving behind lessons rather than wreckage. Infantry are trained to fight without regard for survival, trusting that even in death they serve a greater purpose.

In an age of collapsing empires and fading authority, the Ashen Legion does not fear the fire to come. They welcome it. From ruin, they believe, perfection is inevitable.

Lore
The Architects of Ta hold a singular belief. The galaxy is not governed by chaos or fate, but by arrangement. Worlds fail because their elements are misaligned. Civilizations fracture because their priorities compete rather than converge. To the Architects, these conditions are not tragedies, but design flaws awaiting correction.

Ta stands as the clearest expression of this doctrine. Its surface is shaped by vast golden structures arranged with exacting precision, each city planned as part of a unified planetary system. Nothing rises without purpose, and nothing persists without justification. Every corridor, district, and spire serves a calculated function, contributing to an equilibrium that leaves no excess and tolerates no waste.

Among the Architects, authority is inseparable from evaluation. Governance is conducted through continuous assessment, where influence and productivity are measured, exchanged, and reshaped as circumstances demand. No world is bound to a permanent role. A center of culture may be restructured into a hub of output. A barren holding may be elevated into a seat of coordination.

Now, they move with deliberate purpose. New worlds are not just claimed, but assessed. Systems are altered to better serve the whole, their values reassigned and their functions clarified. All by the grand design.
